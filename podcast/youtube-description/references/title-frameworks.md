# Title frameworks

Five patterns for interview podcasts. Give one option per pattern when generating alternatives. The examples use a made-up guest: Dana Reyes, founder of Fernhill Coffee, on a show for independent coffee shop owners.

**Hard rules for every title:**

- 50 to 65 characters. YouTube allows 100, and long titles get truncated in feeds and on mobile.
- No em dashes. Use `|` or `:`.
- Guest name in the last 30%, so search picks it up without burying the hook.
- Front-load the story angle. Nobody searches for a small show's name.
- No clickbait the episode cannot pay off.

## Pattern 1: story-led

**When:** the guest has a clear before and after.

**Templates:**
- `From {old state} to {new state} | {Guest Name}`
- `How {Guest Name} went from {X} to {Y}`
- `How {Guest Name} {specific achievement}`

**Examples:**
- `From garage roaster to 40 tonnes a year | Dana Reyes`
- `How Dana Reyes rebuilt Fernhill after losing her biggest account`

## Pattern 2: curiosity or question

**When:** the episode reveals a tension or a non-obvious truth.

**Templates:**
- `Why {audience} keep getting {topic} wrong | {Guest Name}`
- `What no-one tells you about {topic} | {Guest Name}, {Role}`
- `Is {common belief} true? | {Guest Name}`

**Examples:**
- `Why most coffee shops lose money on their best seller`
- `Is single origin worth it for a small shop? | Dana Reyes`

## Pattern 3: lesson-led

**When:** the episode has clear takeaways and a teachable structure.

**Templates:**
- `{N} things {audience} should know about {topic} | {Guest Name}`
- `{N} lessons from {achievement} | {Guest Name}`
- `{N} mistakes most {audience} make in {topic}`

**Examples:**
- `5 things shop owners should know about buying green coffee`
- `3 mistakes coffee shops make with wholesale pricing`

## Pattern 4: contrarian

**When:** the guest has a strong, against-the-grain take. Producer notes usually flag it.

**Templates:**
- `What most {audience} get wrong about {topic} | {Guest Name}`
- `{Audience} are wrong about {topic}. Here's why | {Guest Name}`

**Examples:**
- `What most shop owners get wrong about loyalty cards`
- `Why your best barista should not train the team | Dana Reyes`

## Pattern 5: achievement-led

**When:** the guest has a notable number or award.

**Templates:**
- `{Guest Name}: {Achievement or Number}`
- `Inside {achievement} | {Guest Name}, {Role}`

**Examples:**
- `Inside a Roaster of the Year win | Dana Reyes, Fernhill Coffee`
- `Dana Reyes: 40 tonnes a year from one small roastery`

## Output format

For each option, give the character count and one sentence on which signal it leans on and why it fits this guest.

> 1. **From garage roaster to 40 tonnes a year | Dana Reyes** (51 chars): story-led. Surfaces the headline angle from the producer notes, with the name at the end.

## Picking the recommended title

Recommend one. Reference the headline angle from `pre-call-brief.md`, what is distinctive about this guest, and whether the channel needs broad search pickup or deep-niche signal. Small channels tend to do better with story-led or contrarian titles, because those do not rely on anyone knowing the show.
